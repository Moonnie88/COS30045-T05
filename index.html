Choosing an energy-efficient TV

Buying a new TV? Its price tag is only part of the cost. Every year it runs, it adds to your electricity bill. These four charts show why running costs matter, and what to look for in the shop.

Wholesale power prices have more than tripled since 1998

Average yearly spot price paid for electricity in Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and South Australia. Tick a region to add its line. Higher wholesale prices flow through to household bills, so a TV's energy use matters more each year.

Data: Ex5_ARE_Spot_Prices.csv (COS30045 Exercise 5).

At 55", OLED TVs use about 60 kWh more a year than LCD or LED

Mean labelled energy use of 55-inch TVs by screen technology. Comparing one size only takes screen size out of the picture, so the difference comes from the technology itself.

Data: Ex5_TV_energy_55inchtv_byScreenType.csv (COS30045 Exercise 5).

Within the same screen size, TVs with more stars use less energy

Each dot is a group of models with the same brand, screen technology and size, plotted by its average star rating and labelled energy use. Bigger screens use more energy, which hides the pattern when all sizes are mixed. Pick one screen size to see that more stars clearly means lower energy use.

Data: Ex5_TV_energy.csv (COS30045 Exercise 5).

Across all sizes, OLED TVs use the most energy, but most TVs on sale are LED

    Average energy use: mean labelled energy use of all TVs by screen technology. Share of models: how many registered TV models use each technology. LED is the most common at every screen size, and there are no OLED TVs of 32 inches or smaller. Click a slice or a legend item to see its value.

    Data: Ex5_TV_energy_Allsizes_byScreenType.csv (average energy) and Ex5_TV_energy.csv (share of models), COS30045 Exercise 5.

    What this means for you

    Wholesale power prices have more than tripled since 1998, so the energy a TV uses adds to your bills every year you own it. The four charts point to three simple checks in the shop:

    1. Choose the size you need. Bigger screens use more energy. Every TV up to 32" uses under 200 kWh a year, while some over 65" use more than 1,000.
    2. Compare stars within that size. At the same screen size, more stars clearly means less energy (scatter plot).
    3. Check the technology on the label. At 55", OLED uses about 60 kWh more a year than LCD or LED (bar chart). Go by the kWh per year figure on the Energy Rating Label, not the brand.
